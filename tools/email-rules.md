# How every customer email is written

Claude writes only the sentences (greeting, the short intro, the closing). The options, prices, what's included and the terms are filled in by the board, word for word, so the numbers never change.

1. Sound like the owner (me/voice.md). Copy his sentence length, his words, how he opens and signs off. If he writes short, write short.
2. Plain words. A homeowner reads this on a phone.
3. Two sentences at most for the intro. One for the closing.
4. No sales talk: no "elevate", "transform", "pristine", "top-notch", "don't hesitate", "we'd be thrilled".
5. No filler or hedging: no "I hope this finds you well", "just wanted to", "please feel free".
6. No em dashes, no emojis, no exclamation marks unless he uses them.
7. Don't promise anything the quote doesn't say (dates, results, discounts).
8. Mention one real detail of the job (the street, what they asked for) so it reads like he wrote it for them.
